CareConnect

Healthcare appointment booking redesigned for older adults
Role: Lead UX/UI Designer
Timeline: 4 Weeks
Tools: Figma, HTML/CSS/JS

The Problem

Adults 65+ face significant barriers when booking healthcare appointments online. Existing patient portals are cluttered, use confusing medical jargon, have tiny text, and require multiple complex steps. As a result, 43% of older adults abandon the booking process entirely.

"I gave up and just called the office. The website was too confusing." — Margaret, 72

The Challenge: How might we create an appointment booking experience that's simple, clear, and respectful—designed specifically for older adults?

Design Principles

Based on research insights, I established four core principles:

1️⃣
One Task Per Screen: Reduce cognitive load by presenting a single, clear choice at each step.
2️⃣
Plain Language Always: "See a doctor" instead of "Schedule an encounter." No medical jargon.
3️⃣
Trust Through Transparency: Show progress clearly, allow easy backwards navigation, provide reassurance.
4️⃣
Human Escape Hatch: Every screen includes a phone number. If digital fails, human support is one click away.

Accessibility First

Every design decision was made with accessibility at the forefront:

📏
Typography: 20-24px body text, 58px headlines (vs. industry standard 14-16px)
🎨
Contrast: WCAG AAA ratios throughout—tested with color blindness simulators
⚡
Speed: No timers or countdown pressure—users can take their time
🎯
Spacing: Generous white space prevents overwhelming users

Key Learnings

✅
What Worked: Large touch targets eliminated misclicks entirely. Phone number on every screen built trust even if users didn't call.
💭
What I'd Do Differently: Add video tutorials for first-time users. Test earlier with users experiencing cognitive impairments.
🚀
Next Steps: Expand to prescription refills and lab results. Partner with EHR vendors for integration. Conduct 6-month longitudinal study.

Conclusion

CareConnect demonstrates that when we design with empathy and intention, technology can truly serve everyone. By centering the needs of older adults—not as an afterthought, but as the primary users—we created an experience that's not only more accessible, but fundamentally better for all users.

This project reinforced my belief that good UX isn't about fancy features or trendy aesthetics—it's about removing barriers, building trust, and making people feel confident and capable.
